The sensor

Why a drone sees what you can't

Healthy leaves absorb red light for photosynthesis and reflect near-infrared light strongly. A stressed plant reflects less near-infrared, often before its leaves change color. The drone measures both, and a simple ratio turns that difference into a vigor score for every spot in the field.

Flown with a calibrated four-band multispectral camera, RTK positioning and a sunlight sensor that corrects for changing light, so maps from different days compare fairly.

  • 560nm
    Green
  • 650nm
    Red
  • 730nm
    Red edge
  • 860nm
    Near-infrared, invisible to the eye
NDVI = (NIR − Red) ÷ (NIR + Red)

Scores run from −1 to 1. Bare soil reads around 0.1 to 0.2. Dense, healthy canopy reads 0.6 and up.

NDVI is the common starting point. The same flight also produces NDRE, GNDVI, OSAVI and other indices, so if your agronomist prefers a different one for your crop or growth stage, you get that map instead at no extra flight.

The process

How a flight works

Most growers start with one block they're worried about. If the map earns its keep, we add the rest.

  1. Tell me about the field

    Crop, acreage and what you're seeing or worried about. I'll suggest the right timing and which maps will answer your question.

  2. I fly it

    A pre-planned, automated flight at a set height with nothing driving through your rows. I handle the airspace checks and safety.

  3. You get the maps

    A vigor map and a photo map, plus a short summary of where the numbers are lowest. Files open on your phone, load into farm software, or go straight to your agronomist.

  4. Track the season

    Repeat flights show whether a change made a difference and flag new weak spots while they're still small.
